Sixth backup quarterback in four games. Kyle McCord is next for Buffalo's defense.

Kyle McCord will be the sixth backup quarterback Buffalo has faced in four games when Miami visits Sunday (1 PM ET, Highmark Stadium), per the Democrat and Chronicle. The first five combined for 367 yards, no touchdowns and four interceptions — but Buffalo's secondary came out of the Jets win with Benford, Alford and Williams all hurt.

Kyle McCord is next. When the Miami Dolphins (1-8) visit the Buffalo Bills (8-1) on Sunday, Nov. 22 at 1 PM ET at Highmark Stadium, McCord will be the sixth backup quarterback Buffalo has faced in four games, per the Democrat and Chronicle, and Sunday will be just his second career NFL start. It will also mark the third straight week the Bills have faced a quarterback who had never thrown an NFL regular-season pass before November, per the same reporting. Buffalo has won five straight. The quarterbacks standing across from it keep changing mid-game anyway.

The revolving door started on Nov. 1, when Baltimore Ravens quarterback Lamar Jackson injured his throwing thumb on the first offensive snap, per the Democrat and Chronicle. Backup Tyler Huntley suffered a toe injury in the second quarter, which thrust undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano into his first NFL game. The following week, Minnesota Vikings quarterback Kyler Murray missed the game with a hamstring injury; Carson Wentz started, posted an 8.9 passer rating and was replaced by Max Brosmer in the fourth quarter, per the same reporting. On Sunday against the Jets, Geno Smith exited twice — first to be evaluated for a concussion, then with an ankle injury — and fourth-round rookie Cade Klubnik made his NFL debut in relief. Three weeks, three starters gone, five backups already through the door. McCord makes six.

The five who came before him produced a combined line that explains Buffalo's month: a 48.1 percent completion rate, 367 passing yards, zero touchdowns, four interceptions and a 41.4 passer rating, per the Democrat and Chronicle, while Buffalo outscored its opponents 90-28 in that stretch. The Associated Press put the defensive run in clock terms: Buffalo has not allowed a touchdown since Derrick Henry's 2-yard run for Baltimore on Nov. 1 — a span of 168 minutes, 18 seconds — and has not trailed in 217 minutes, 13 seconds, dating to the second quarter of the Oct. 18 win at Las Vegas. A defense that averaged 28.5 points allowed through its first six games has given up a combined 28 points in its past three, per AP, and after forcing six turnovers in the first six games, it has forced eight in the past three. Defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard's unit is not just surviving backup quarterbacks. It is burying them.

McCord, though, is not arriving as a punchline, and his first start is the reason why. He set a franchise record in his NFL debut on Sunday, completing 17 of 32 passes for 209 yards — the most passing yards by a Dolphins quarterback in his debut — in Miami's 24-22 loss to Indianapolis, per the Democrat and Chronicle. He did not throw a touchdown or an interception, ran for a touchdown, and lost a fumble in his own end zone that Indianapolis recovered for a touchdown, per the same reporting, while Miami nearly erased a 21-7 second-half deficit. Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley was encouraged by his demeanor, per the Democrat and Chronicle: "He was composed, he didn't get rattled." The college resume backs up the calm: McCord, 24, led the nation with 4,779 passing yards at Syracuse in 2024, an ACC single-season record, with 34 touchdown passes for a 10-3 team, per the same reporting. Drafted in the sixth round by Philadelphia in 2025 and traded from Green Bay to Miami in August for a 2028 sixth-round pick, he now gets his second start back in upstate New York — against a defense that has not surrendered a touchdown since the first quarter of November, per the Democrat and Chronicle.

Here is the part of this story that is actually about Buffalo, though: the defense that built that streak came out of the Jets game limping. Cornerback Christian Benford was ruled out with a groin injury in the fourth quarter after entering the game questionable with the right wrist injury he suffered against Minnesota, per the Democrat and Chronicle; Benford has 33 tackles and eight pass breakups in eight games this season, per the same reporting. Nickel cornerback Dee Alford was evaluated for a concussion twice, was cleared to return once, then was ruled out, and must clear the NFL's concussion protocol before he can play against Miami, per the same reporting. Linebacker Dorian Williams left in the fourth quarter with a calf injury and did not return — after recording a team-high nine tackles, two quarterback hits, a pass defended and his first career sack, per the Democrat and Chronicle. Safety Cole Bishop had his right arm and shoulder examined late but played 68 of 71 defensive snaps with no injury designation announced, per the same reporting; cornerback Maxwell Hairston was shaken up late and walked off under his own power, and linebackers Terrel Bernard and Joe Andreessen both headed to the X-ray room after the game, per The Buffalo News via the Democrat and Chronicle, with neither receiving an in-game designation. The one full-strength debut helped: safety Amani Hooker, five days after being acquired from Tennessee, played all 71 defensive snaps against the Jets with seven tackles and a pass defensed, per AP. Wednesday's first practice report of Dolphins week is the first real clue about who is actually available — and until it posts, every one of those names is a report, not a ruling.

Miami's own list is longer, and it explains how McCord got this job. The Dolphins placed starting quarterback Malik Willis on injured reserve on Nov. 10 with an injury that originated in his groin and progressed to his core; the earliest he can return is Week 14, per the Democrat and Chronicle. Running back De'Von Achane suffered a torn ACL on Sept. 27 and will miss the rest of the season, and defensive lineman Zach Sieler was placed on injured reserve Nov. 10 and is also out for the season, per the same reporting — three of Miami's six original team captains are on injured reserve. Miami will also arrive with a brand-new kicker: the Dolphins waived Zane Gonzalez on Monday, one day after he missed field goals from 47 and 51 yards and an extra point in the 24-22 loss at Indianapolis, per Reuters via Field Level Media, which noted Miami is looking for its third placekicker of the season. A 1-8 team, on its backup quarterback and its third kicker, walking into Highmark Stadium in late November — that is the matchup. On paper.

The paper, however, is not the prize. The Associated Press reported that Buffalo need not apologize for the caliber of its competition, and that clinching a sixth division title in seven years seems a matter of when, not if, given the state of the AFC East's quarterback ranks — a picture that got clearer Monday, when Patriots starter Drake Maye was believed to have suffered a long-term lower leg injury that will reportedly sideline him for several weeks and possibly the rest of the season, per the Democrat and Chronicle, with New England already trailing Buffalo by four games in the loss column. What is still unanswered, per AP, is how Buffalo stacks up against the league's contenders — and those answers start arriving immediately after Miami: a five-week stretch that opens with Kansas City at home on Thanksgiving night and ends with a Christmas Day game at Denver, with trips to New England and Green Bay and a home game against Chicago in between, all against teams that entered Week 11 at .500 or better, per AP. Josh Allen said it himself after the Jets win, per AP: "We're not playing our best football right now." So Sunday's job is twofold — extend a touchdown-less streak that is now older than some of the quarterbacks it has faced, and get the secondary that has to survive Mahomes, Love, Williams and Nix through the afternoon healthy enough to try. Handle McCord, protect the streak, and let the real exam start on Thanksgiving. Go Bills — and get every Dolphins-week practice report in your inbox every morning with The BNC Blitz.
