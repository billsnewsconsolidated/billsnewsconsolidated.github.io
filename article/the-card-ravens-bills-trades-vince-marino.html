The Card: 3 trades I like for Ravens at Bills, and the matchup numbers behind them

I'm Vince, and this is the one column on this site where we talk about the numbers that move money: matchup grades, real stats, and the prices on Kalshi — the regulated exchange where you trade on game outcomes instead of betting against a house. Every pick I make here gets logged, win or lose, in public. No locks, no 'can't lose,' no deleting the misses. If a handicapper won't show you his record, he's selling you a feeling. I sell homework.

This week's homework starts where the whole channel started this week: Lamar Jackson. On the Bills News Consolidated YouTube channel we broke down why a returning Lamar might be great news for Buffalo — and the market angle is the same story from the other side. Jackson has missed Baltimore's recent games with an ankle injury, the reports say he's expected back Sunday, and Jackson himself said earlier this month that pushing it would leave him 'restricted' — his word was 'a statue.' A statue doesn't beat you with his legs. Price the quarterback who's actually playing, not the one on the poster.

Matchup one: James Cook against this Ravens front. Cook is averaging 5.34 yards per carry on 627 rushing yards — those are PFF-verified engine numbers, not vibes — and Baltimore just got run on in a 35-13 loss to Cincinnati where they led 13-0 at halftime and never scored again. Buffalo comes in 5-1, at home, off a bye. A rested run game against a defense that fades is the oldest edge in football.

Matchup two, and it's the one that should scare Bills fans, because The Card doesn't do homer math: Christian Benford. PFF has quarterbacks posting a 124.4 passer rating when they target Benford this season. That's the worst kind of number, and it's exactly where a smart opponent attacks. If Lamar's ankle lets him stand in the pocket, Baltimore's passing game against Buffalo's corners is a real path for the Ravens. Buffalo's defense ranks 31st in yards allowed and 28th in scoring. Anyone selling you a Bills blowout is skipping this paragraph.

Matchup three is my favorite number of the week: 48-0. That's what Baltimore has been outscored by in the second halves of its last two games. Zero points scored after halftime, twice, including blowing that 13-0 lead Sunday. Teams that can't finish are the best trade in sports, because the live market keeps pricing the first-half team.

And the player angle, straight from this week's channel breakdown of Keon Coleman: 19 catches, 283 yards, 3 touchdowns, zero drops, and a 138.0 passer rating when Allen targets him — with touchdowns in three straight games during his hot stretch. That's not a flier. That's a trend with a stat sheet.

So here's my card for Sunday, Nov. 1, Ravens at Bills, 1 PM ET at Highmark. One: Buffalo to win — home, rested, and facing a limited quarterback; check the live price on Kalshi before you trade, because the number moves with every Lamar headline. Two: the second-half fade — if Baltimore leads or hangs early, the live second-half market is where their last two games say the value is. Three: Coleman to find the end zone again — the trust numbers (138.0 when targeted) say Allen looks for him when it matters.
